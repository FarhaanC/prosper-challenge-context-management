## Building the rules module (5–6 Oct)

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22 | **Text mode is our own small loop**, not a text-only Pipecat pipeline. It reuses the same stage definitions and `clinic` code, and copies the 3 Pipecat Flows behaviours that matter: history wiped on a stage move, a second AI pass after every tool call, one tool call at a time. | Pipecat is built around live audio; pushing about 90 typed conversations through it means extra plumbing, slower runs and harder token counting. **Risk:** small differences from Pipecat. **Safeguard:** one shared stage definition; the demo scenarios are also run by voice. |
| 23 | **Day and time wishes are structured fields** (days like "Thursday" or "2026-10-13", plus morning/afternoon), not free text. The AI fills them in. | The AI is good at turning "next Thursday morning" into fields; code that parses spoken dates is brittle. *During review rounds 2–4 the code drifted from this and started reading phrases; rounds 4–7 brought it back and extended the rule (see 37–40).* |
| 24 | ~~"Near X" ranks by distance; "the X one" means only X~~ **Replaced by 39** (near is a yes/no). Still true: a name that fits several locations ("Mission", "North") makes the agent ask. | Guessing between Mission Bay and Mission District is exactly the mistake to avoid. |
| 25 | **Rule 6 is enforced by design.** Every option is doctor + location + time together; the final check refuses a booking with no location; in the fake schedule a doctor is at one location per working day. | The caller can't end up with a doctor but no settled location, and a doctor can't be double-booked across sites. |
| 26 | **When a choice changes, the old doctor and place become wishes, not choices.** The next search honours them if they still fit, and says so plainly if they don't. | Nothing is kept or dropped silently (decision 11). |
| 27 | **"New patient" means new to the clinic group**, not new to that doctor. | The catalog has one flag per doctor and per visit type, and no history of who saw whom. The simplest reading that fits the data; stated as an assumption. |
| 28 | **A referral is stored as a specialty** and covers every referral-needing visit type in it. | The catalog has no referral detail. Simple and explainable. |
| 29 | Booking-card preferences gained `preferred_provider_id` / `preferred_location_id` (for 26) and the structured `days` / `time_of_day` (for 23). | Exact ids avoid re-asking "which Dr. Ramirez?" after the caller only changed the time. |
| 30 | **No new packages** (apart from `tzdata` for time zones on Windows, added in round 1). Matching uses Python's standard library. | Nothing extra to install or break. |

## Review rounds (6–7 Oct)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 31 | **The newest wish wins.** Wishes are applied strongest first: the latest change, then other things the caller said (doctor, place, time, language), then choices carried over. A wish is applied only if something survives it. Every note is checked against all valid options, so it's true. | Farhaan, round 1 | Both round-1 reviewers found the old order gave false "nothing available" notes. |
| 32 | **Returning patients give full name and date of birth.** Nothing is looked up without both, and every miss gets the same reply. | Farhaan, round 1 | "Mr. Wilson" found James Wilson, and "Robert Tyler" got Robert Taylor's record. The same reply for every miss means the agent never reveals whether a name exists. |
| 33 | **"The Mission" is ambiguous: the agent asks** (Mission Bay or Mission District). | Farhaan, round 1 | Guessing is the baseline's mistake. |
| 34 | **Fix the smaller spec items too:** specialty words ("dermatologist"), street names, a neighbourhood used as a name. | Farhaan, round 1 | Cheap, and callers say these. |
| 35 | **Notes are plain records:** a kind (e.g. `place_ambiguous`) plus the sentence. | Farhaan, round 2 | The tool layer can react to the kind (ask "which one?") without reading the sentence. |
| 36 | **Identity, step by step:** try again; then the caller spells their last name (third try); then offer to register them as a new patient or put them through to staff. Same name *and* birthday → staff. After 3 unusable answers in a row, the same offer. | Farhaan, rounds 2–3 | People share names and speech-to-text mishears; nobody should be asked forever. |
| 37 | **Turning doctors down is its own clear instruction** (`turn_down_doctors`: given ids, the doctors on offer, or the current doctor). The code no longer reads "someone else" or "not Dr. Chen". | Farhaan, round 4 | Rounds 2–4 kept finding bugs in code that read the caller's sentence. That broke decision 23's principle: the AI understands speech; the code follows instructions. |
| 38 | **Days and times are plain fields only** (back to decision 23): each day is a weekday, "today", "tomorrow" or a date; the time of day is "morning" or "afternoon". Anything else is refused with a note saying how to send it. | Farhaan, round 5 | The code had started reading phrases like "Thursday or Friday, morning please", so "Thursday works" was refused. |
| 39 | **"Near" is a yes/no the AI passes**, next to a place name. | Farhaan, round 6 | The last phrase reading left was spotting "near"-type words. |
| 40 | **The rule for what comes in from the AI**, stated in `clinic/__init__.py`. Names (doctors, places, languages) are matched forgivingly against our own records, because the AI doesn't know them. Two fixed word lists are read: "no preference" words (treated as empty) and words no name has, like "not" (refused). Everything else is a plain instruction, and anything else is refused, never guessed. | Builder, from 37–39; worded after round 7 | One sentence that can be defended, and checked by reading the code. |
| 41 | **A word like "clinic" or "center" in a place name counts only if it's part of that place's own name.** "Sunset Clinic" works; "Chinatown clinic" is not quietly read as "near Chinatown". | Fresh verifier, round 7 (the builder disagreed and lost) | A rule built from all eight names only fit this catalog, and it read phrases. |

A language we have no record of gets a note listing the languages we do have. | Builder, rounds 5–6 | "Spanish French" or "Chinese" must never be guessed, and every note must be true. |

## Wiring the stages and tools into the voice agent (8 Oct)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 43 | **Every change of wish goes through one tool, `change_choice`.** There's no separate `find_options` tool. In the booking stage, every change rebuilds the stage with fresh options. (Replaces the `find_options` tool in the plan.) | Farhaan | Two tools that look alike ("find something else" and "change something") invite the AI to pick the wrong one. One way in means one behaviour to test. |
| 44 | **This step gets the full independent check:** a separate fresh tester and a fresh reviewer, in parallel (as in round 1 of the rules module). | Farhaan | The tool layer is new code that the AI talks to directly; it deserves the same scrutiny as the rules. |
| 45 | **A simple call log now; token counts and the live panel with the editor.** Each call writes one file (`backend/call_logs/`, not committed): one line for every stage move (with the instructions the AI was given) and every tool call (what was sent, what came back, the booking card). | Farhaan | It lets us check a real voice call line by line today, and it's what the editor's inspector panel will read. |
| 46 | **"Can I speak to a person?" works in every stage** (`hand_off` is always available). | Farhaan | A caller who asks for a person mid-booking should get one, not be pushed on. |
| 47 | **Wishes said early travel with the stage's main tool.** `start_booking` and `choose_visit_type` take the same wish fields (doctor, place + near, days, morning/afternoon, as soon as possible, language), so one tool call carries everything said so far. In stages 1 and 2, `change_choice` records the change without moving stage: it can't skip the identity check, or wipe stage 2 while the caller is choosing. | Builder | Each extra tool call costs about 1 s of silence. Moving stage on a change only makes sense once there are options to rebuild. |
| 48 | **Bookings are saved one per line in `backend/bookings.jsonl`** (not committed) and reloaded when the agent starts, so a saved time is never offered again. A damaged line stops start-up with a message naming the line, rather than being skipped. One call at a time is assumed (it's a demo). | Builder | Plain text that can be read by eye, and appending a line can't damage earlier bookings. |
| 49 | **The tools are plain Python in `backend/call/`; Pipecat stays in `agent_builder/`.** The tool list (the "tool registry") holds each tool's description, fields and the stages it can move to. The builder checks, when the agent file loads, that every tool exists, every stage a tool can move to exists, and every `{placeholder}` in the instructions is one the code knows. | Builder | Decision 22: the voice agent and the typed tests must run the same stage and tool code. Load-time checks turn a typo in the agent file into an error at start-up, not a broken call. |
| 50 | **The goodbye stays with the AI, reworded.** The persona's "use a tool" rule now applies until the goodbye; the end stage says "speak only, don't call any tool"; hand-offs say "a member of staff will call you back", not "putting you through". **Known remaining risk:** Pipecat offers the always-available tools in every stage, so if the AI still calls one in the end stage, the call ends without the goodbye. | Farhaan (review R2, R13) | A natural goodbye was preferred over a fixed sentence spoken by the code. The wording removes what pushed the AI towards a tool. |
| 51 | **One change_choice call can carry several changes.** Its fields are the same wish fields start_booking takes (doctor_name, place + near, days, time_of_day, soonest, language), plus visit_type (and specialty). Every field sent is applied; "any" drops a wish; an empty field counts as not sent. (Replaces the `what` / `new_value` shape.) | Farhaan (review R3, test T3) | "Friday afternoon at Midtown" is one breath. With the history reset on every move, a second part left for a second call was silently lost. |
| 52 | **Visit types are listed before stage 2 starts, when the specialty is clear.** start_booking takes an optional specialty, which the AI picks from the 21 names now shown in stage 1. Stage 2 opens with that specialty's visit types (`{visit_types}`), marked bookable or not. | Farhaan (review R5) | Saves one AI pass of silence (3 → 2 after identification, within decision 9's budget). It's the same trick as the pre-fetched options, and the AI still picks the specialty; the code never reads the caller's words. |
| 53 | **Four small behaviour changes:** turn_down_doctors works in every stage (by name before options exist); wishes said during a failed identity try are kept; a field a tool doesn't have is refused, not ignored; once a visit type is accepted, the card's visit description is that visit's name. | Farhaan approved the builder's proposals (review R4, R7; tests T4, K1) | Each closes a way for something the caller said to be lost silently, or for the card to contradict itself. |
| 54 | **A refused value changes nothing; a visit the caller can't book is not a refused value.** If choose_visit_type is refused because the visit can't be booked, the wishes sent with it (doctor, days...) still count. | Farhaan (review R12, won't fix) | They are the caller's own words; dropping them would lose what was said. |
| 55 | **The order of a call is enforced by the code.** Once the call is booked or handed off, only answer_question and hand_off work. Tools that need the caller refuse until start_booking succeeds. Whether a change moves the call depends on the card (no caller yet → stay), not on a stage's name. | Builder (tests T5, T6; review R6) | The AI only sees each stage's tools in a voice call, but the typed test loop (decision 22) and edited agent files shouldn't depend on that. |
| 56 | **Each stage is told what just happened.** One line written by the code (`{just_happened}`), e.g. "The caller asked for a different kind of visit". Stage 2 thanks the caller only when that line says they were just identified, and the AI says one short sentence before calling a tool. | Farhaan (round-2 review S13) | After a stage reset the AI can't tell a fresh start from a change of mind, and might thank the caller by name mid-call or leave them in silence. |
| 57 | **Wishes said early aren't undone by later, smaller steps.** Before options are on offer, turning a doctor down only adds to the "not these doctors" list (it isn't a doctor change). Doctors turned down before any visit was agreed survive a change of visit. "As soon as possible" on its own isn't a new day or time wish. | Builder (round-2 review S1–S3) | Each was a way for something the caller said to be silently lost, which decision 11 rules out. |
| 58 | **The greeting is spoken by the code the moment the call connects** ("Hello, you've reached the clinics' scheduling line. How can I help you today?"), and the AI waits for the caller (`respond_immediately: false`, Pipecat's own setting, now in our agent format). | Farhaan (first voice call) | The first call had about 6 s of silence before the first word, 3.7 s of it the AI writing a greeting. A fixed line needs no AI, and saves one AI call per call. |
| 59 | **New patients' names are spelled back for confirmation** before start_booking ("F, A, R, H, A, A, N, is that right?"). | Farhaan (first voice call) | Speech-to-text heard "Farhan" for "Farhaan": spoken words carry no spelling, and a new patient's name is saved as heard. Returning patients are matched forgivingly and saved as the record has them. |
| 60 | **Nothing is promised before the options.** Stages 1–2 must not say or suggest that a doctor, place or time is available, or that anything is being booked; stage 2 doesn't ask about preferences. | Farhaan (first voice call) | In the first call the agent said "I'll register you for that skin check with Dr. Garcia", and an extra "any preferences?" question let Dr. Chen replace Dr. Garcia before the code's "no Dr. Garcia does skin checks" note could be said. |
| 61 | **Speech-to-text is English only.** | Farhaan (second voice call) | Left to guess the language sentence by sentence, it transcribed the caller's name, said on its own, in Russian ("Фархан Чидов"). The agent only speaks English. |
| 62 | **A particular day is sent as "today", "tomorrow" or its date; a weekday name means any such weekday** (in the next two weeks). Said in the tools' day description. | Farhaan (second voice call) | "Can we do it for tomorrow?" was sent as "Friday", which the code rightly read as any Friday, so the caller was offered next week's Friday. |
| 63 | **Stage 1 passes a doctor's name on unchecked**: no looking the doctor up, and no "which one?", before the visit is chosen. | Farhaan (second voice call) | In the second call stage 1 asked "which Dr. Garcia?" and moved quietly to dermatology, so the code's clear "none of our Dr. Garcias do skin checks" was never said. Checking the doctor against the visit is the code's job, in the options stage. |
| 64 | **Silence, hold and pocket dials:** after 20 s of silence the agent asks "Are you still there?"; after 15 s more it says a short goodbye and hangs up. | 65 | **Calling back to change or cancel a booking stays a hand-off to staff,** explained in solution.md. | Farhaan (after the voice calls) | There's no safe way to find a new patient's booking: they aren't in the patient records, and we don't take their date of birth. | 66 | **The code speaks the read-back after choose_option,** instead of the AI repeating it. *(Built 11 Oct.)* | Farhaan (after the voice calls) | It saves one AI pass (about 0.7 s) on that turn, and the caller hears exactly what will be saved, every time. |
| 67 | **In the booking stage the agent says a short line ("Let me check") before searching again.** *(Built 11 Oct.)* | Farhaan (after the voice calls) | The search doesn't get faster, but the caller isn't left in silence; the same instruction already worked in stage 2. |

## Silence, the code-spoken read-back, and the editor (11 Oct)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 69 | **Every line the code says (the greeting, the read-back) goes through the builder's own "say" action,** not Pipecat Flows' `tts_say`. Cost: speech too soft for the voice detector now cuts the agent off when its final text arrives, a fraction of a second later. |
| 82 | **The agent doesn't say the caller's name in the identify stage**, not even to thank them; it thanks them by name once the name is saved. | Farhaan (same) | It said "Thank you, Perhan" (a mishearing of Farhaan) before the name was spelled. |
